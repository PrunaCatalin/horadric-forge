Rune types

Every rune is either Ritual or Invocation. Ritual runes generate Offering when their condition is met; Invocation runes spend Offering to fire an effect. A socketed pair needs one of each — two Ritual runes or two Invocation runes do nothing together. This is separate from the cube: in the cube a rune is only crafting material and its type is irrelevant.

Where runes drop

Runewords are never found — you craft them here. What drops is runes, and a few of them can be targeted. Put one point into Lair of Runes, the first node on the left branch of the Lair Boss War Plan tree; without it these bosses are not a rune source at all. Even with it the drops are chance, not guaranteed, and Blizzard publishes no rates — the table below is community testing.

The shortcut worth knowing. Jah has a boss. Climbing to it instead costs 64 Mal, and Mal is the one rune on the ladder with no recipe and no boss — it only drops. So for anything needing Jah, farm the Beast in the Ice rather than the ladder. The same does not apply to Ber or Mal, which no boss targets.

For raw Mal and the other high runes, Season 15's Waking Nightmares is the activity guides name most often, with Baal and Kurast Undercity also mentioned. Figures like "one Ber every 60 to 90 minutes" come from single guides and are anecdotal (?).

Getting the keys

A key is never spent to enter a lair or fight the boss. It is spent on the Hoard afterwards, which is where the rune is. Lair Bosses need Torment I or higher to attempt at all.

Lair Key→ Hoard of Varshan, Grigoire, Beast in the Ice, Lord Zir or Urivar →1x Greater Lair Key
Greater Lair Key→ Hoard of The Butcher, Duriel, Andariel or Harbinger of Hatred →the rarer runes

So the initiate bosses are the key factory and the greater bosses are what you spend on. Greater Lair Keys also come from War Plan Key Spoils, and a Stygian Stone converts into any Greater Key one for one. Plain Lair Keys come from Helltide chests, Pangs of Duriel, War Plan rewards, Nightmare Dungeon caches and Astaroth affixes. Players report Greater Keys only flowing properly from Torment 6 up, which is anecdotal, not an official threshold (?).

Stygian Stones are a crafting material, converted at the Alchemist. They come from chests in The Pit, where the chance scales hard with tier — 5% at tier 1, 10% at 40, 18% at 100, 30% at 200 — and also from initiate Lair Boss chests and Infernal Hordes material chests. Since patch 2.2.0 they are no longer needed to summon Lair Bosses, which is why older guides describe them doing something else entirely. The Alchemist's exact conversion list has been renamed across patches, so read what it offers rather than trusting a guide's names (?).

One loop covers Enigma. The Beast in the Ice is both the Jah source and a Greater Lair Key source, and the Greater Key opens The Butcher, who is the Vex source. So the same farm feeds both runes Enigma still needs: Jah straight from the Beast, and Vex from the Butcher the Beast paid for. Eight Vex make the Ber.

The 13 Iconic Mythic Uniques

"Mythic" means two different things. These 13 are the Iconic Mythics: fixed items that drop at Mythic rarity and exist only as Mythics. Separately, any Ancestral Unique at 900 Item Power can be raised to Mythic rarity with the Season 15 cube recipe, keeping its affixes, Tempering and Masterworking. That is why a Stone of Jordan can read Ancestral Mythic Unique Ring without being on this list — it is a Legacy Unique that was upgraded, not an Iconic Mythic. The Season 15 Legacy Uniques are in the second table below.
No Iconic Mythic belongs to a particular boss. All twelve Uber bosses draw from the same pool of thirteen, at the same rate, so a drop is random across the whole list. If a build guide still prints a line like "Drops from: Duriel, King of Maggots" under an item, that is a leftover from when Duriel was the only Uber boss — it does not mean the item is tied to him now. One guide does claim Belial has far better odds with Andariel and Duriel 50% above the rest, which contradicts the equal-rate sources, so treat any boss-specific advantage as unsettled (?).

The consequence is practical: since no boss improves your odds for a specific Mythic, farm whichever one is cheapest and fastest for you. Targeting one item means crafting it with Resplendent Sparks instead, or upgrading an Ancestral Unique in the cube.

There is no recipe for a specific Mythic any more. Older guides still list per-item rune recipes (Selig as 2 Sparks + 6x Vex + 6x Thul + 6x Tun at the Jeweler). That system is gone. Three routes work now:

1. Drops. Torment I or higher. Any Uber/Lair boss can drop any Mythic, reported at the same rate for each, around 2%. Duriel and Andariel are often named as the best targets.
2. Random Mythic Unique — 3x Resplendent Spark. Gives one of the 13 at random; you cannot pick. Sources disagree on the vendor, Blacksmith or Jeweler.
3. Horadric Cube, Season 15 — Upgrade Direct turns that item Mythic, keeping its affixes, Tempering and Masterworking. Sources disagree on the cost: Maxroll lists an Ancestral Unique at 900 Item Power plus 5 Resplendent Sparks, PTR guides say Item Power 850+ plus Pandemonium Fragments. It does not hand you a named Mythic Unique from this list. The result carries the Crafted tag, and you can only equip one Crafted item at a time, so a second cube-upgraded Mythic cannot go on alongside the first. Dropped and vendor-crafted Mythics have no such limit.

Salvaging a Mythic returns 1 Resplendent Spark. Mythic Uniques cannot be traded.

The rest of the cube recipes

RecipeMaterialsNotes
Sigils are not a cube recipe. You craft them at the Occultist, not here. A Nightmare Sigil costs 60 Sigil Powder plus 4,000 gold, and salvaging one returns 20 Sigil Powder (down from 25 in patch 3.2.0). An Infernal Horde Compass costs 500 Sigil Powder (down from 666) and a random Magic Tribute costs 100 (down from 200). The cube only takes sigils as an Amalgamation input.
Escalation Sigils and the Reserve affixes. A sigil's affixes roll when the sigil is created and are final — there is no reroll, in the cube or anywhere else. So you cannot craft one to come out with Materials Reserve. You get an Escalation Sigil from the cube recipe above, from Horadric Strongrooms (Torment 1 and up, best odds at Attunement tier 6), from the Horadric Knowledge reputation track, or from bosses while the Recurring Nightmare War Plan node is active. What rolls on it is luck.

You do not need an Escalation Sigil for this affix at all. Ordinary Nightmare Sigils roll Materials Reserve themselves, confirmed on Blizzard's own forums alongside Gem Reserve, Gold Reserve and Strongroom. So the cheap route is the Occultist: craft a Nightmare Sigil for 60 Sigil Powder and 4,000 gold, check the affixes, salvage it for 20 Powder back if it is wrong, repeat.

What the cube does to a sigil's affixes. No guide states it, so this was tested in game. Five Nightmare Sigils went in, from five different dungeons across three regions. Every one of them carried Horadric Strongroom; their second affixes were all different. Out came a sigil for a sixth dungeon, in a fourth region, carrying Horadric Strongroom and one affix that had been on none of the inputs.

Read that as: the dungeon is rolled fresh, the slot the inputs disagreed on is rolled fresh, and the affix every input shared carried through. If that holds, five sigils that all carry Materials Reserve produce an Escalation Sigil with Materials Reserve, and the affix stops being a lottery.

A second run put three competing rules against each other, with five sigils sharing nothing across all five. Two rules died. A majority does not carry — an affix on three of the five did not appear. Position does not matter either: the sigil placed first in the grid was the only one with Materials Reserve, and neither of its affixes came through. The output's two affixes were unrelated to the inputs, and the dungeon was again unrelated to all of them.

What is still open is whether the shared affix really carries or trial 1 was luck. A given affix lands on a sigil by chance roughly one time in seven, and the second run showed exactly that happening — an affix sitting on one input out of five turned up on the output, in a run where nothing could have carried. So treat the rule as a working theory worth acting on, not a fact. The runs are logged in TESTS.md in the repository.

Two things the cube will not do for you: there is no preview of the output's affixes, so you only learn them after committing. But the recipe panel's Transmutation (n) counter is a live validity check — it reads (1) when your items match a recipe and (0) when they do not, with the Transmute button greyed out to match. Load first, read the counter, then commit.

The control is inside the dungeon, not on the sigil. Each time you finish a floor you choose the next portal, and every portal shows its own affixes — take the one carrying the Reserve you want. The Branching Pathways War Plan node adds two extra portals per transition, which is the only real way to raise your odds of being offered a given Reserve. It still does not guarantee one appears.

Every Reserve affix stashes 8 Horadric Chests in the dungeon. Materials Reserve holds gold, basic and legendary crafting materials, Bundled Herbs, Angelbreath, Veiled Crystals and Forgotten Souls. The others are Gold, Obols, Gem Fragment, and the per-gem Reserves (Amethyst, Diamond, Emerald, Ruby, Sapphire, Skull, Topaz) — those last ones are what you match to a Soul Splinter when farming splinters.

Every dungeon affix

A Nightmare Sigil always rolls exactly one positive and one negative affix — one from each pool, never two from the same one. That is confirmed by Maxroll and visible on every sigil tooltip. So the odds of landing a particular Reserve are set by the size of the positive pool alone, not by the whole list.

The pools are not flat. If all 22 positives were equally likely, a given one would roll about 4.5% of the time. Dungeon Delve turned up on three sigils out of five in one of our test batches, which a flat pool makes roughly a 1-in-1000 event. Some affixes are plainly far more common than others. Nobody publishes the weights, so the only way to measure them is to tally your own stash.
